The Thirty-Second Rule

Which two defenses should you rotate through the first six weeks? After four independent lines of attack, one number decides it — and no model built here improves on reading it off the board.

Panel 4,222 defense-weeks Backtest 2018–2025, walk-forward Pairs scored 496 Updated 19 Aug 2026

Verdict

The opponent's implied team total is the entire signal. Defensive form, opponent-offence quality, opposing-QB identity and prior-season defensive ratings each add essentially nothing on top of it.

The benchmark was named before any model was fitted: each week, start whichever rostered defense faces the lowest opponent implied total. That rule takes thirty seconds. Every model that knows anything the rule does not ranks worse than it.

Draft one defense very late if you want one, and stream the other slot.

Claims are tagged measured inferred or rejected. A critic pass re-derived 181 numbers from source data and corrected 14, including one data bug. Corrections are marked in the full report rather than applied silently.

A circular claim, caught and corrected

This report originally read "the model beats the trivial rule by +0.00 pp". That was circular: the script selects the best model by accuracy, and the winner is a ridge fit on the single market variable, which ranks identically to the rule by construction. Stated non-circularly, the best model that knows anything extra scores 60.95% against 61.01%. Adding features makes ranking worse — harsher than what was first published, not softer.

02

Why weeks 1–6, honestly

The window was chosen because forecasting gets harder further out. That is true — but not for the assumed reason.

93 / 93Games in weeks 1–6 with a 2026 betting line
12 / 165Games in weeks 8–18 with one
~6.5 wkHalf-life of preseason defensive knowledge, CI [1.8, >50]

The predictor that works physically stops existing after week 6. That alone justifies the window. What does not justify it is preseason team quality: those ratings are weak everywhere, at r ≈ 0.14 and 55% accuracy even inside weeks 1–6. Preseason at its best (55.1%) loses to the market at its worst (58.6%). measured

The sanity check that the decay method works at all: the market shows no decay, best fit by a constant. A forward-looking price should not decay the way a backward-looking rating does.

This table is one defense plus a plateau

New England appears in eight of the top ten pairs — not a claim about the defense, but a schedule artifact: they draw the Jets in week 6 and the Raiders in week 5. The top ten span 2.3 points in total, the eight New England pairs span 1.1 — which is the partner's entire measurable contribution — and the best New England-free pair is only 1.9 back.

Against a 33-point outcome width, choosing correctly within the top ten is worth about 7% of one pair's own spread. Do not agonise over the partner.

The same pair tops both the floor and the ceiling list, which looks like a finding and is not. Across all 496 pairs r(p10, p90) = 0.978 and the p90−p10 width is 28–36 for every one of them: they are near-translates of each other. The model has no defense-specific quality term, so it cannot express a floor-versus-ceiling trade-off. True but hollow. inferred

Every one is an opponent implied total between 16.5 and 18.0. That is the whole board, and there is no second criterion worth applying.

Note the spread. The single best matchup available all season has a median of 8 and a 90th percentile of 17, with an 11% chance of an outright bust. Even a perfect matchup is a coin flip between a dull week and a good one. This is why the distribution is the product and the point estimate is not.

On double counting, which the brief warned about

Implied team total correlates 0.820 with the spread and 0.576 with the game total, and implied_total == (total + spread) / 2 to machine precision. A defense facing a low implied total, a large spread and a low opposing passing-TD prop has not received three pieces of evidence. It has received one, three times.

The first version of this comparison set six weeks of D/ST against a full season of a skill player, which is not a comparison. The critic pass built the missing curve: pick value truncated at week 6, with replacement re-derived on six-week totals. The six-week share turned out to be 59.5% of full-season value, not the 37.5% naive proration assumes — it had to be computed, not reasoned.

The direction survives but was overstated, and it reverses at the tail. Net −1.6 to −3.6 before pick 145, −1.1 at picks 145–156, and +0.3 after pick 157. A very late defense roughly breaks even; anything earlier does not. inferred

It is still not perfectly clean: median usable points above replacement is 0.0 in every bucket, so this is a lottery mean against a near-certainty, and the D/ST side is censored at week 6 rather than complete.

07

What to actually do

  • Do not draft a defense early. Nothing about defensive quality predicts well enough to justify it, and defensive touchdowns are literally random.
  • Draft one defense very late, or none. The second drafted defense only breaks even after pick 157.
  • Stream on opponent implied team total, and only that. It beats every model built here, including the ones that know defensive form, opponent offence quality and the opposing quarterback.
  • Target 16.5–18.0 implied opponent totals. That is the whole board.
  • Do not agonise over the partner. Within the top ten pairs, the choice is worth about 7% of a single pair's own spread.
  • Expect variance. The best matchup available has a median of 8 and an 11% bust rate. Streaming is a distribution, not a plan.
